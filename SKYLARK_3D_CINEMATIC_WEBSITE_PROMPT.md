## Core Creative Decision

Do not build a normal seafood company website.

Build an interactive 3D cinematic journey where the user follows one shipment from origin to export. The website begins with a large ship leaving the Indian coast at around 10 AM. The sunlight is high enough to feel active and commercial, not dawn or night. The sea is blue-green, the deck is wet, the cranes are awake, workers are moving, and the business already has momentum.

The visitor gets two primary route choices:

1. Ocean Route: from the Bay of Bengal, through catch, landing, inspection, processing, freezing, cold storage, and export.
2. Pond Route: from Andhra aquaculture ponds, through water sampling, batch traceability, harvest, residue testing, processing, packing, and export.

Both routes converge into one standard: QC, certification, frozen chain, documentation, reefer container, vessel departure, and global delivery.

## New Cinematic Story

Use this story instead of simply adapting the PDF hero film.

### Story Name

`The Voyage of the Promise`

### Story Premise

At 10:00 AM on the Andhra coast, a refrigerated container is waiting at the edge of a port. It will eventually travel on a large vessel out of Visakhapatnam, but the website rewinds through the two possible histories inside that container.

One history begins in the open Bay of Bengal: wild catch, ice, landing, inspection.

The other begins inland at aquaculture ponds: logged water, controlled inputs, batch identity, harvest.

The visitor chooses which origin to explore. Whichever path they choose, the site proves the same point: origin may differ, but the standard does not.

### Narrative Arc

1. The Ship Waits
   - Start on a large export vessel and reefer container at the port in bright late-morning sunlight.
   - The camera is already moving. The ocean is calm but powerful.
   - Minimal text: `A shipment is never just a shipment. It is a history under seal.`

2. Choose The Origin
   - Split the world into two living 3D portals:
   - Left: open ocean with fishing vessel, nets, ice crates, seabirds optional only if tasteful.
   - Right: geometric aquaculture ponds with bund roads, water-sampling technician, harvest crates.
   - CTA labels: `From The Ocean` and `From The Ponds`.

3. Ocean Route
   - User enters a sea-level cinematic sequence.
   - Show a fishing vessel moving across the Bay of Bengal under 10 AM sun.
   - Product is iced immediately.
   - The scene transitions to Vizag harbour, intake, inspection, accepted/rejected sorting.
   - Tone: elemental, direct, powerful.

4. Pond Route
   - User enters an aerial and ground-level aquaculture journey.
   - Show rectangular ponds, water sampling, feed/input log, batch tag, harvest crates.
   - Emphasize traceability before the shrimp ever reaches the plant.
   - Tone: controlled, methodical, audit-ready.

5. The Plant
   - Both paths converge into a stainless-steel processing environment.
   - Show washing, deshelling, de-heading, peeling, deveining, grading, sorting.
   - Product forms appear as elegant overlays: `HOSO`, `HLSO`, `PUD`, `PTO`.

6. The Test
   - Slow the tempo.
   - Enter the residue lab and QC station.
   - Show sample preparation, instruments, inspection light, documents, clearance.
   - Copy: `Trust is not a claim. It is a result.`
   - Mention EU/US residue limits only if verified. Otherwise say `tested to buyer and destination-market requirements`.

7. Freezing The Morning
   - IQF tunnel, block freezing, blast freezing, glazing.
   - Make this the most visually beautiful part: frost, clean water glaze, cold vapour, crystalline highlights.
   - Copy: `Freshness is protected by temperature, time, and discipline.`

8. Packed For The World
   - Cartons, vacuum packs, labels, scales, pallets, cold storage.
   - Label overlays should show real data fields but may use sample values until final facts are supplied.
   - Fields: product name, species, size grade, batch number, net weight, origin, country of origin, compliance.

9. The Seal
   - Reefer container loading, temperature set, documents assembled, container sealed.
   - Camera returns to the large ship from the opening.
   - The ship leaves the Bay.
   - Final line: `From this shore to yours, every step protects a promise.`

## Visual System

### Time Of Day

Everything should feel like 10 AM to afternoon:

- Clean sunlight
- Blue-green ocean
- Bright sky, not night
- Light haze from humidity
- White glints on water
- Warm Indian coastal highlights
- Cool stainless interiors
- Crisp cold-storage whites and blues

Avoid:

- 4 AM darkness
- storm drama
- gloomy noir treatment
- generic stock seafood imagery
- beach-resort tourism mood

### Colour Palette

Use a premium but varied palette:

- Bay blue: `#0E6F8E`
- Deep water: `#063544`
- Sea green: `#2FA889`
- Sunlit brass: `#D7A84F`
- Ice white: `#F4FAFA`
- Stainless grey: `#AAB8BC`
- Coral accent: `#E36F52`
- Ink text: `#102024`

Do not let the website become one-note blue. Use sea green, brass sunlight, stainless, and coral product details to break the palette.

### Typography

Use a premium editorial/industrial pairing:

- Headings: a strong modern sans such as `Sora`, `Space Grotesk`, or `Neue Haas Grotesk` equivalent.
- Body: `Inter`, `Manrope`, or similar.
- Numerals and labels: tabular, compact, technical.

No negative letter spacing. No huge text inside small cards. Use hero-scale type only in the hero and major cinematic beats.

### Layout Character

- Full-bleed 3D canvas behind the page.
- Overlay text should be sparse, confident, and readable.
- Use panels only when the user needs to compare data or select products.
- Avoid decorative floating card stacks.
- Do not put cards inside cards.
- Use tight professional controls: icon buttons, segmented route toggle, timeline dots, tooltips, and product filters.

## Interaction Design

### Origin Selector

At the first choice point, create two large interactive 3D options:

- `From The Ocean`
  - Hover: ocean surface brightens, fishing vessel moves, ice sparkles.
  - Click: camera dives toward boat/deck and starts ocean route.

- `From The Ponds`
  - Hover: pond grid highlights, water sample ripple expands, batch tag appears.
  - Click: camera flies inland over aquaculture ponds and starts pond route.

Add a persistent toggle after selection so users can switch route without restarting the whole page.

### Scroll Behaviour

- Scroll should feel like a cinematic camera dolly through a continuous world.
- Each section should have one clear camera move, not constant random movement.
- Use `prefers-reduced-motion` support.
- On mobile, reduce camera complexity and keep content legible.

### Product Explorer

Create a product module with four shrimp forms:

- `HOSO` - Head On Shell On
- `HLSO` - Headless Shell On
- `PUD` - Peeled Undeveined
- `PTO` - Peeled Tail On

Each option should show:

- 3D/product image or stylized render
- Form explanation
- Typical buyer use case
- Available size grades
- Pack/export-ready status

Do not make unverified claims about certifications or markets. Use cautious placeholders where facts are pending.

### RFQ Flow

The contact section should feel like a buyer tool, not a generic form.

Fields:

- Product form
- Size grade
- Origin preference: Ocean / Pond / Either
- Destination market
- Estimated quantity
- Packaging preference
- Required certification or documentation
- Buyer name, company, email, WhatsApp

## Implementation Requirements

### Suggested Stack

- Vite
- React
- TypeScript
- React Three Fiber
- Drei
- GSAP ScrollTrigger or Framer Motion plus scroll progress

### Performance

- Lazy-load route scenes.
- Use Draco or Meshopt compression for GLB assets.
- Texture budget should be controlled.
- Use responsive render scaling.
- Provide static fallback imagery for low-power/mobile devices.
- Add `prefers-reduced-motion` mode.
- Ensure first meaningful paint is fast even if 3D loads progressively.

### Accessibility

- Every 3D route choice must also be a real HTML button.
- Maintain keyboard navigation.
- Preserve readable contrast over video/canvas.
- Provide reduced-motion and non-canvas fallback content.
- Do not hide key information inside canvas only.

### Mobile

- Use simplified camera paths.
- Keep buttons large.
- Route choice should stack vertically.
- Product cards should be stable with fixed media aspect ratios.
- No overlapping text, especially over canvas.

- The first screen immediately communicates Skylark Exim, shipping scale, seafood export, India, and premium trust.
- The origin choice is visually obvious and emotionally compelling.
- Ocean and pond routes feel different but converge into one standard.
- The product forms are clear and buyer-useful.
- Quality/residue testing is treated seriously.
- The ship/export finale feels earned.
- Mobile is polished, not just squeezed.
- No section looks like a generic SaaS landing page.
- No text overlaps or disappears over the 3D canvas.
- The site can be presented to a seafood exporter or international buyer without embarrassment.
