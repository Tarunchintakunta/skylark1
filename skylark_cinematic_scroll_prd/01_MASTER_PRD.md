# Skylark Exim Cinematic Scroll Website - Master PRD

## Product Vision

Create the best cinematic seafood export website for Skylark Exim: a scroll-controlled WebGL voyage from the Bay of Bengal to global markets.

The user should not feel like they are reading a company page. They should feel like they are moving through a premium export film inside the browser.

## Primary Story

The website follows a group of fishermen who board a large fishing vessel and travel into the Bay of Bengal. The scroll controls the vessel's departure, sea journey, fishing sequence, catch handling, return to port, processing, freezing, export container loading, container-vessel departure, and global route reveal.

The hero seafood is:

- Swordfish
- Tuna

Other fish and seafood appear as supporting products. Shrimp remains important for the pond/aquaculture and product-form chapter.

## Main Message

`Caught in the Bay. Protected by the cold chain. Proven by documentation. Delivered to the world.`

## Brand Message

`Two Origins. One Standard.`

## Final Line

`From this shore to yours, every step protects a promise.`

## Experience Rules

The entire website must be a scroll animation.

Every section must be part of one cinematic timeline:

- Scroll moves the camera.
- Scroll changes scenes.
- Scroll reveals text.
- Scroll controls object motion.
- Scroll changes lighting and atmosphere.
- Scroll moves from ocean to port to plant to export.
- Scroll ends with a globe and global shipment routes.

Avoid:

- Static page sections.
- Generic seafood brochure design.
- SaaS card layouts.
- Dark finance-site copying.
- Tourism visuals.
- Restaurant styling.
- Random decorative animation.

## Time And Mood

The story happens in bright morning-to-afternoon daylight.

Use:

- 10 AM coastal sunlight.
- Blue-green Bay of Bengal water.
- Moving clouds.
- Sunlit waves.
- Humid Indian coastal haze.
- Clean industrial port light.
- Cold blue-white processing interiors.
- Bright QC lab light.
- Crisp freezing and cold storage visuals.

Avoid:

- Night.
- 4 AM darkness.
- Storms that make the operation feel unsafe.
- Excessive danger.
- Dirty decks or dirty processing.

Adventure is welcome, but trust and clean handling must remain intact.

## Core User Journey

1. User lands on a cinematic port/ocean scene.
2. Fishermen board the vessel.
3. Compass/data overlay appears.
4. Vessel travels into the Bay of Bengal.
5. Nets are cast.
6. Swordfish and tuna are caught.
7. Catch is placed into onboard chilled storage.
8. Vessel returns to Vizag port.
9. Product is transferred into cold-chain containers.
10. Product enters the facility.
11. Fish are inspected, cut, washed, graded, frozen, packed, and stored.
12. Shrimp product forms and pond traceability are shown as supporting chapters.
13. QC and residue testing prove trust.
14. Cartons move into reefer export containers.
15. Containers travel to a large container vessel.
16. A sky view becomes a globe view.
17. Route lines move from India to international markets.
18. The user reaches the RFQ/contact flow.

## Required Interaction

### Scroll

Scroll is the primary controller.

The site must have one master scroll timeline that controls:

- Camera position.
- Camera target.
- Vessel movement.
- Ocean waves.
- Net casting.
- Catch reveal.
- Compass overlay.
- Port return.
- Container transfer.
- Facility movement.
- Product-form transitions.
- QC document reveals.
- Freezing effects.
- Container seal.
- Globe route animation.

### Compass Interface

Create a cinematic compass/data interface during the ocean journey.

Show:

- Time.
- Sea temperature.
- Onboard storage temperature.
- Wind direction.
- Route heading.
- Bay of Bengal location.
- Catch status.
- Distance from port.

The compass should feel like a premium navigation HUD, not a sci-fi game UI.

### RFQ

The final buyer action should be an RFQ form.

Fields:

- Product interest: Swordfish, Tuna, Shrimp, Other.
- Shrimp form if relevant: HOSO, HLSO, PUD, PTO.
- Size grade.
- Destination market.
- Quantity.
- Packaging preference.
- Required documents.
- Buyer name.
- Company.
- Email.
- WhatsApp.
- Message.
